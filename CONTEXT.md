# Mise Project Glossary (CONTEXT.md)

Single source of truth for Mise-specific terminology. When a term is used in
chat, code, or docs, it MUST match the definition here. New terms get added
on resolution during /grill-with-docs sessions.

## Domain (Restaurant Operations)

- **Tenant** — a restaurant **business** that uses Mise (one customer = one tenant). Has at least 1 Branch. The tenant — not the branch — is the unit the business is **managed** at: purchasing, costing and accounting are ultimately run centrally, so every operational number must roll up to a business-wide view, with the branch as the drill-down rather than the starting point.
- **Branch** — a physical restaurant location of a Tenant. A Tenant has 1+ branches. It is where stock physically exists, and therefore the unit at which quantity and **cost** are *measured* — two branches hold two different piles bought on different days at different prices. Measuring per branch and managing per tenant are separate things; a business-wide figure is an explicit roll-up of branch figures, never a silent average.
- **Department** — internal grouping within a Tenant (e.g., Bar, Kitchen, Bakery). Optional feature — opt-in via tenant.enable_departments.
- **Main department** — default department auto-created on tenant signup (H.1.2). Used when departments are disabled.
- **Owner** — top-level role on a Tenant. Has all permissions including billing.
- **Manager** — full operational role, no billing access.
- **Purchaser** — can create PRs/POs/GRs and manage suppliers.
- **Kitchen staff** — can create PRs, count stock, confirm GRs.
- **Accountant** — view-only on operations + edit on financial.

## Master Data

- **Supplier** — vendor that sells products to a Tenant. Has VAT/WHT defaults.
- **Category** — 3-tier classification: account (COGS/OpEx) → accounting_section (Food/Beverage/etc) → group (Meat/Seafood/etc).
- **Product** — a purchasable or producible item. Two types:
  - **RAW** — bought as-is from supplier (e.g., whole salmon).
  - **PREPPED** — produced from other products by prep. Made in one of **two mutually exclusive ways** (ADR 0021 Q1): from a single `parentProductId` with a [Yield] (portioned salmon fillets — one input, one output), **or** by a [Production recipe] (น้ำพริกเผา — chilli, garlic, shallot, oil and palm sugar). Never both: they are two notations for the same fact, and holding both invites two numbers that disagree. Parent chains are allowed up to depth 5 (Decision #58), enforced at write-time by `assertParentValid` (live-only ancestor-walk + descendant-DFS + visited-set; rejects self-ref + cycles); see ADR 0007. Delete is blocked while live children — or live recipes — reference it.
- **Yield** — output/input ratio for PREPPED products (e.g., 80% yield = 1kg raw → 800g prepped). NOT loss percent. It measures **conversion loss only** — what the knife and the heat take between RAW and PREPPED. It is a property of the product and the method, so it is the same on a good week and a bad one. Food that went off in the fridge is **not** yield: that is [Waste], and it has a date, a quantity and someone who can act on it. Lowering yield to cover spoilage hides a recurring loss inside a constant, where nobody can see it or fix it (ADR 0017 Q3).
- **Servings per recipe** — จำนวนที่ต่อสูตร: how many portions one writing of a recipe produces. Defaults to 1, so a dish cooked to order needs no thought; a curry cooked by the pot says 20. ⚠️ **Not a [Yield], though both are spoken of as "yield" and both are divisions.** Servings splits a pot into plates; yield accounts for what the knife takes. A recipe can need both.
- **Yield math** (Decision #59) — two divisions, applied in order and never confused:
  `per_serving = recipe_qty ÷ servings_per_recipe` — then — `raw_qty = per_serving ÷ (yield_percent / 100)`.
  NEVER `qty × (1 + loss%)`. Read backwards, a [Production recipe] whose inputs total 300 g for a 250 g output *is* a yield of 83.3%; that percentage is computed at read and never stored, and is shown only when the inputs share a dimension it can be computed from.
- **Joint products** — ผลผลิตร่วม: one input yielding **several** valuable outputs — a beef primal giving 0.8 kg of steak cuts, 0.1 kg of fat that becomes ข้าวผัดมันเนื้อ, and 0.1 kg discarded. **Not supported**, and not for want of a table: there is no single [Yield] that describes it (80% pretends the fat is rubbish, 90% pretends fat and steak are worth the same per kilo), and the real question is how the 500 ฿ divides, which is a **policy and not a fact**. Decision #6 ("joint allocation by market value") is the intended policy and has never been designed; `product.target_market_price` and `product.expected_yield_g` were reserved for it in Sprint 1 and have had no reader or writer since. See pending Feature 7.
- **Type-change guard** — `Product.type` was freely editable through Sprint 1–4 because nothing read it. ADR 0021 Q13 ends that: **PREPPED → RAW is refused while a production recipe outputs the product**, and **RAW → PREPPED re-checks the depth of every recipe using it** before the write, naming the recipe that would overflow. A product still used by any recipe cannot be deleted. Same family as ADR 0005's base-unit-change guard and ADR 0006's multi-unit-referenced-by-mapping guard — the latter is closed for recipes by ADR 0021 Q17.
- **Unit** — measurement (g, kg, ml, l, ชิ้น, ฟอง, etc.). Pre-loaded as unit_template.
- **Base unit** — the single canonical unit a Product's stock is internally tracked in. Every Product has exactly one (a ProductUnit with isBase=true, toBaseRatio=1). User-picked from unit_template, not normalized to SI — cross-dimension math uses unit_template.toSiRatio instead. **Part 10 makes this the stock baseline**: every `stock_movement.qty` is stored in this base unit (ADR 0011 Q1), normalized at the action layer via `ProductUnit.toBaseRatio` before INSERT, so Balance = `SUM(qty)` needs no JOIN or ratio math and past movements are immune to later ratio edits.
- **Default buy unit** — the unit a Product is ordered/purchased in by default (ProductUnit.isDefaultBuyUnit). For a single-unit Product it equals the base unit.
- **Liquid density** — g/ml standard density for liquids (water=1.000, milk=1.030, cooking oil=0.910). Used to convert between WEIGHT and VOLUME for the same product; the math itself lives in the Sprint 2+ cost engine — **Part 7d is data-capture only**. Stored as either a foreign key to `liquid_density_template` **OR** a per-product `densityGPerMlOverride`, never both — XOR is enforced at zod (`.superRefine`), at the action-layer cleanse, AND by a Postgres CHECK constraint. Templates are **system-global, non-deletable, no tenant scope**; admin updates propagate via FK to all linked products on next read (no snapshot, no re-link needed). Density is only meaningful when `primaryDimension !== "COUNT"`; the UI hides the section, zod rejects, and the server cleanses density to null for COUNT-primary products (mirrors ADR 0007's PREPPED/RAW cleanse-before-validate ordering). See ADR 0008.
- **Multi-unit** — a product can have multiple units (e.g., milk in ml AND l AND ขวด).
- **Additional unit** — any non-base ProductUnit on a Product. Converts to the base unit via a product-specific `toBaseRatio` and shares the Product's dimension (same dimension as the base — cross-dimension is handled by Liquid density, not units). May use a custom packaging name not in unit_template (e.g. กระสอบ, ลัง, ขวด); the base unit, by contrast, must come from unit_template.
- **Unit source** — provenance of a ProductUnit's *name*: `system` (name matches a unit_template entry) or `custom` (free-text packaging name). Records where the name came from only; the `toBaseRatio` is always product-specific (1 ขวด of brand A milk ≠ brand B).
- **Supplier-Product Mapping** — a price-list entry linking a Supplier to a Product: the supplier's own item code/name, the unit it is ordered in (`orderUnit`, a ProductUnit of that product), current price, and procurement terms (min-order qty, lead-time, preferred flag), effective from a **calendar date** (`effectiveFrom`; the "when entered" timestamp lives separately in `createdAt`). Append-only **time-series**: entering a new price *supersedes* the previous one (the old row's `effectiveTo` closes; a new open row opens with `effectiveTo = null` = current). Overlapping date ranges are rejected; future-dated prices are allowed. Scoped either **tenant-default** (`branchId` null) or **branch override** (`branchId` set). **Preferred** = the default source for a product — at most one per product per branch-scope (app-enforced like Default buy unit). The PO / cost-engine consumer is Sprint 2; Part 8 is data-capture only. See ADR 0009.
- **Orphan mapping** — a Supplier-Product Mapping whose parent Supplier or Product has been soft-deleted. Tolerated by design (the data layer does not enforce referential *liveness*); surfaced via an "All" list filter, never crashes a read. In Part 8.5 the term narrows in the restore context to **kept-live orphans** — mappings the user chose to *keep* (`deletedAt IS NULL`) during the Part 8 cascade-with-control delete, as opposed to the cascade-soft-deleted ones; only kept-live orphans participate in restore (see Orphan-mapping reactivation).
- **Hide-not-delete** — in Part 8, "ลบ" a mapping (or soft-deleting its parent Supplier/Product, which cascades to the mappings after a blast-radius confirm) is a **soft-delete: hidden from active lists + the Sprint-2 PO consumer, but retained for audit/history** — financial records are never destroyed. **Part 8.5 adds the inverse** — Restore-on-recreate (see below). See ADR 0009.
- **Restore-on-recreate** — Part 8.5: a soft-deleted Product is **un-deleted at product-create time**, not via a separate trash screen. Typing a name (3+ chars, 400 ms debounce) runs a server-side Fuzzy match over the tenant's soft-deleted products; selecting a candidate re-uses its existing **ID** (idempotent `UPDATE … SET deletedAt=null` guarded by `deletedAt IS NOT NULL`), so every field reverts as-is and kept-live orphan mappings recover meaning through the unchanged FK. Resolves a Same-day overwrite and an optional `newSku` on SKU collision. No new audit surface (reuses `updatedAt` + the Part 8 history viewer). See ADR 0010.
- **Fuzzy match** — `pg_trgm` trigram similarity search used by Restore-on-recreate over soft-deleted products, scored `GREATEST(similarity(name), similarity(sku))` at **threshold 0.4**, top 10 (5 shown + "ดูเพิ่มอีก 5"). Each result carries a coarse badge (>0.7 ตรงกันมาก / 0.5–0.7 ใกล้เคียง / 0.4–0.5 อาจเกี่ยวข้อง — the raw decimal score is never shown) and a `matched_on` tag (name vs sku). Backed by two partial GIN indexes (`gin_trgm_ops`) `WHERE deleted_at IS NOT NULL`.
- **Orphan-mapping reactivation** — Part 8.5: when a Product is restored, its **kept-live** orphan mappings regain operational meaning automatically (the FK never changed — no separate write). Cascade-soft-deleted mappings stay deleted (respecting the earlier Part 8 cascade choice; reviving them = manual re-creation). If kept-live orphans exist, restore **forces a price-review step** (Option C, no threshold): per-mapping radio defaulting to ใช้ราคาเดิม, or อัปเดต to supersede price/min-qty/lead-time. See ADR 0010.
- **Same-day overwrite** — Part 8.5 (Option ε): when a restore-time mapping price update targets a row whose `effectiveFrom = today` (Bangkok UTC+7, Decision #60), the row is **updated in place** rather than superseded — a zero-duration price that never backed a real transaction has no audit value. Older rows (`effectiveFrom < today`) follow the ADR 0009 supersede pattern (close old `effectiveTo`, insert new open row). A deliberate narrow exception to the append-only time-series rule. See ADR 0010.

## Procurement

- **PR (Purchase Request)** — internal request to buy something, raised by a **department** and approved by a manager. Pre-purchase document. Only meaningful once a tenant runs more than one department, so it is **deferred to Sprint 3+** (ADR 0012).
- **PO (Purchase Order)** — formal order sent to supplier. **A sent document, not a draft intention**: while `DRAFT` it is freely editable, and from `SENT` onward it is immutable — the supplier holds a copy, so amending means cancelling and issuing a new one. See ADR 0012.
- **Order unit** — the unit a PO line is *ordered* in (กระสอบ, ลัง), as it will appear on the supplier's invoice. Distinct from the product's **Base unit**, which is what stock is counted in.
- **Line snapshot** — the price, unit name and unit ratio **copied onto a PO line when it is sent**, rather than followed through a live reference. A PO must still mean in five years what it meant on the day it was sent, even if the product's units or price list have moved since. ADR 0012.
- **GR (Goods Receipt)** — ใบรับสินค้า: the record of one physical delivery, and **the only thing that turns a purchase into stock**. A two-state document — `DRAFT` while the delivery is still being counted (posts nothing), `CONFIRMED` the instant it writes its `PO_RECEIVE` movements to the Ledger, increments each PO line's `qty_received` and recomputes the PO status. May reference a PO **or stand alone** (see Standalone GR). Every quantity converts to the base unit with the **PO line's frozen `to_base_ratio`**, never a live ProductUnit lookup (ADR 0012 Consequence 1). See ADR 0013.
- **Standalone GR** — a Goods Receipt with `purchase_order_id = null`: the fresh-market run nobody raised an order for. Its lines snapshot the **live ProductUnit** at receipt time — not a weakening of the Line-snapshot rule, because there is no earlier document whose meaning could drift; the receipt *is* the originating record. ADR 0013 Q1.
- **GR void** — the only way to correct a `CONFIRMED` GR: never an edit (the Ledger forbids it), but a transition to `VOIDED` that appends a **reversal line** per original line into the *same* document — negative qty, `reversal_of_item_id` pointing at what it undoes — each producing its own `PO_RECEIVE_REVERSAL` movement. `qty_received` is decremented back and the PO status recomputed. The Compensating-entry doctrine applied to a document. ADR 0013 Q6.
- **GR shortage** — received less than PO. The PO simply stays `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED`; department allocations pro-rate by ratio (H.3, largest-remainder tiebreak, lowest id).
- **GR excess** — received more than PO. **Never blocked** — the goods are already in the kitchen; recorded in full, sets `has_discrepancy`, and requires a note on the line (Decision #56, H.3). No tolerance band.
- **ปิดรับ (closed short)** — manually closing a PO the supplier will not complete: sets `status = RECEIVED` and stamps `closed_short_at/by/reason`. Nothing infers it — without the button a PO short by 2 kg would stay in the open-orders read forever. ADR 0013 Q8.
- **Allocation** — the split of one PO or GR line's quantity **across departments** (cost-centre attribution) — *not* the matching of GR lines to PO lines, which is a direct parent reference. Every line's allocations must sum to the line's quantity. While departments are off, a line has exactly one allocation row, "Main".
- **Mirror trigger** — DB trigger that updates dependent rows when PR/PO/GR confirmed (H.2). **Not built** — the allocation sum is enforced in the application instead, until multi-department allocation is reachable (ADR 0012).

## Inventory

- **Stock count** — ใบนับสต๊อก: a **document** recording what was physically found on the shelf, line by line, and the only thing that reconciles the Ledger with reality. `DRAFT` while counting (freely editable — a working sheet), `CLOSED` the instant it posts each line's variance to the Ledger, `VOIDED` if the whole count was wrong (reversal lines appended into the same document, never an edit). A line means the product **was counted**; no line means it was not touched, and a counted **0** is a real observation, not an absence. Who counted is recorded **per line**, because a count is usually split between people. At most one open count per branch. See ADR 0015.
- **Waste** — ของเสีย: stock that physically left because it could not be used — it went off, it was dropped, it was cooked wrong, a customer sent it back. Recorded as a **single dated event** the moment it happens (no draft, no shift sheet): one thing thrown away, one entry, posted to the Ledger immediately. Waste is deliberately narrower than "stock that disappeared": an unexplained shortage is [Count variance], trim loss is [Yield], and a staff meal is neither.
- **Staff meal** — food eaten by staff. **Not waste**: it is a sale that collected no money, and its cost belongs on the labour/welfare side of the accounts, not in the food-waste figure. Costing one needs sales and recipes; both exist from Part 21 onward, so it becomes buildable in Part 23. It will post [CONSUMPTION] like a sale does, distinguished by its own `source_type` rather than by a movement type of its own.
- **Par level** — the quantity of a product a **branch** wants to have on hand. Set per (product, branch) because a branch that is out of pork is out of pork whatever the business holds elsewhere; entered in any unit, stored in the base unit. It **suggests, it does not order**: falling below par puts the product on a list, and a human decides. Counting is **monthly**, so between counts the figure a par check reads is only as good as its age — every row therefore carries when it was last confirmed, and the drift it accumulates is reset by the next count rather than growing without end. A product below par is listed even when an order is already on its way — the row then says which supplier, how much and when it is due, because an order placed and never chased is the failure nobody notices until service (ADR 0017 Q6).
- **Count variance** — counted minus expected, per line. `qty_expected` is **snapshotted when the line is saved** (not at close, and never recomputed): it is a record of what the system believed at the moment someone stood at the shelf, and a receipt keyed in later must not silently change a count that was already investigated. Its value in baht is **not** stored — that is the cost engine's answer and improves whenever the cost does. Reported separately from spoilage: an unexplained variance points at theft, mis-keying or bad receiving, which is a different conversation from food going off.
- **Ledger** — Part 10's `stock_movement` table: the **immutable, append-only** history of stock changes. Insert-only — no `UPDATE`, no `DELETE`, no `deletedAt`, no update/delete logic. It is the single source of truth for stock; nothing else stores a running quantity. Modelled on a general ledger / bank statement: you never edit a past line, you post a new one. See ADR 0011.
- **Movement** — one row in the Ledger: a single stock change of `qty` (signed `Decimal(15,3)`, `+` = in / `−` = out) in the product's **Base unit**, at a `branch` (NOT NULL), of a given `type` (`PO_RECEIVE` / `PO_RECEIVE_REVERSAL` / `ADJUST_GAIN` / `ADJUST_LOSS`, plus `TRANSFER_OUT` / `TRANSFER_IN` and their two reversals from Part 18 — the `PO_*` types are written only by a GR confirm / GR void, ADR 0013, and the `TRANSFER_*` types only by a [Transfer], ADR 0018), pointing at its origin **polymorphically** (`source_type` + `source_id`, no FK). Exactly one movement per **source key** — the pair `(source_type, source_id)`, not the source row: a [Transfer] line legitimately posts two movements because its two ends carry two different source types over the same id. The sign is bound to the type by a DB `CHECK`. Supersedes the older generic "stock movement" definition. See ADR 0011.
- **Adjustment** — Part 10's `stock_adjustment`: the manual **source** of a Movement — a user-entered count correction (up = `ADJUST_GAIN`) or count-down / spoilage / waste (`ADJUST_LOSS`), tagged with a `reason` (recount / spoilage / damage / other) and the as-entered unit + qty (preserved for audit; the signed base-unit value lives on the Movement). Creating an adjustment inserts the adjustment **and** its movement in one transaction. Waste has no dedicated table in MVP — it is an `ADJUST_LOSS` with `reason = spoilage/damage` (Q10). See ADR 0011.
- **Transfer** — การโอนระหว่างสาขา: a **document** moving stock from one branch to another. The goods belong to the **receiving** branch from the moment they are dispatched — they are going there — so both ends of the move are recorded at once and the document's status says only whether a human at the far end has confirmed them. Deliberately **not** a purchase (nothing was bought, no money left the business) and **not** an adjustment (nobody corrected anything). Recording three people is the point: who sent, who drove, who received. See ADR 0018.
- **In transit** — stock that has left the sending branch and has not been confirmed at the receiving one. It is **not a place**: there is no in-transit balance and no pseudo-branch holding it. It is a **state of the document**, and the goods it describes are already counted at the destination.
- **Transport loss** — ของหายระหว่างขนส่ง: the gap between what was dispatched and what was counted on arrival. Distinct from [Waste] (nothing spoiled) and from [Count variance] (there *is* a document, and it names the driver who accepted the goods). Recorded against the transfer that lost it, so the question it raises has an addressee.
- **Compensating entry** — how the Ledger corrects a mistake: never by editing or deleting a Movement, but by posting **new** movements (a reversal, then optionally a replacement). All rows stay visible and the net Balance is correct. This is the immutability trade-off (one mistake → up to 3 rows), accepted for financial integrity (ADR 0011 Q4/Q7).
- **Balance** — current (or `asOf` a date) stock of a `(product, branch)` = `SUM(qty)` over its movements. Never stored — computed in realtime on a composite index; returned as `Decimal`, serialized to string at the view layer (Pitfall #20). A negative balance is **allowed** (never blocks a write) but flagged with a UI warning + red "ต้องตรวจสอบ" badge (ADR 0011 Q8/Q9).
- **occurred_at vs created_at** — a Movement carries **two** timestamps: `occurred_at` = *business time* (when stock actually changed; **backdatable** within `[today−90d, today]`, Bangkok UTC+7 via `computeBangkokToday`) and `created_at` = *audit time* (when the row was inserted; immutable). Cost calc orders by `(occurred_at, created_at)`. Keeping them separate is what makes backdated entries both cost-accurate and auditable (ADR 0011 Q5; Decision #60). **Part 13 makes `occurred_at` a true instant** (a GR records the minute the delivery arrived), so a date-only query bound now expands to the **Bangkok** day it names, not the UTC one — otherwise a 06:00 delivery would count against the previous business day (ADR 0013 Q4).
- **Stock movement** *(legacy generic term — see **Movement** / **Ledger** above)* — originally "log of all stock changes (CONSUMPTION, GR, WASTE, TRANSFER, ADJUSTMENT)". Part 10 makes it concrete for the MVP subset. Of the generic list, WASTE became a **source** rather than a type (ADR 0017 Q1), TRANSFER became four types of its own (ADR 0018 Q4), and CONSUMPTION arrived in Part 22 as a type with a document of its own (ADR 0022).
- **CONSUMPTION** — the Movement type for stock that left to be cooked and eaten. Named for **what happened to the stock**, not for what caused it, so a sale and (later) a [Staff meal] share the type and are told apart by `source_type` — the same shape `ADJUST_LOSS` already has, serving an adjustment, a count variance and a waste log. Deliberately **not** `RECIPE_CONSUME`: that phrasing claims the word a production movement will need. **Written from Part 22 onward**, whose source is a [Sales consumption run]; its reversal is a type of its own because giving stock back at today's price would let importing a file twice change what the stock is worth (ADR 0021 Q12, ADR 0022 Q6).
- **Yield-correct consumption** — selling a dish explodes its recipe down to what is actually held in stock and consumes that, dividing by [Yield] at every PREPPED step. Until [Production] exists, the explosion goes **all the way through to RAW**: a PREPPED product is a way of writing a recipe, not something the system believes is in the fridge (ADR 0021 Q11).
- **Sales consumption run** — การตัดสต๊อกตามยอดขาย: the **document** that turns one branch's sales for one business day into stock leaving the Ledger. It is a deliberate act, not a side effect of importing the file: a shop may have the sales long before it has the recipes, and a recipe that cannot be resolved must not be able to sink a good import. Posting a day that was already posted **voids the whole day and posts it again** rather than topping it up — the same act a re-import performs, so there is one way to do it and not two. See ADR 0022.
- **Consumption coverage** — how much of a day's sales the run could actually turn into stock movements, expressed as a **share of revenue** rather than of dish count, because the question behind it is how wrong the cost of goods sold is, and a ฿20 water is not a ฿300 steak. A dish counts only if it posted **whole**: anything unresolved beneath it — no recipe, a PREPPED input with no yield, a component menu with no recipe of its own — leaves the whole dish out and names why. Low coverage warns and never blocks, but no figure computed from a partly-posted period is ever shown without it.
- **Cancelled order** — บิลที่ถูกยกเลิก: a sale the POS reversed, arriving as a negative row. Whether its ingredients were consumed depends on something no file records — whether anyone had started cooking — and both answers are common. It is therefore **the shop's own setting**, presented with its consequence and an example rather than assumed: treat it as cooked (stock leaves; a count later shows a surplus, which is itself the evidence orders were cancelled) or as never cooked (the row is subtracted). See ADR 0022 Q3.
- **Production** — บันทึกการผลิต: making a batch of a PREPPED product — consuming its inputs and raising its own stock. **Not built.** Its absence is why nothing can raise a PREPPED balance today, and why counting น้ำพริกเผา reports a gain every time; the screens that touch it say so rather than leaving the user to find out. It is also what ADR 0017 was waiting for when it refused `PREP_LOSS` as a waste reason. It is a Part of its own: aiming for 3 kg and getting 2.8 is not yield, a batch may carry an expiry, and someone has to record it.

## Sales & POS

- **POS Sync** — pulling sales transactions from POS systems (Square, Foodify, Wongnai, etc.).
- **Diff-and-resolve** — *(not built)* sync strategy where Mise compares POS data against local DB and resolves differences (Section B). It was designed for a POS that pushes **recipes**, and no POS in scope does; ADR 0021 Q3 removed it along with the three-layer mirror. The mechanism found a real customer elsewhere — branch-to-branch recipe propagation (ADR 0021 Q9, deferred) — so it was aimed at the wrong party rather than wrong in itself.
- **Mirror** — *(not built, see above)* Mise's local copy of POS data. What exists instead is narrower and typed: a [Menu] mirrors the POS's **identity and name**, not its internals.
- **Menu** — เมนู: a dish that can be sold, and the thing a sale points at. Its identity is the **POS code**, never the name, so a rename, a stray space or a dropped character changes nothing (ADR 0019 Q7). A menu may carry a [Recipe], and a menu may itself be an ingredient of another menu's recipe — that is a [Set menu]. Distinct from [Product]: a product is bought or produced and held in stock, a menu is sold. Deleting one is blocked while a set still contains it.
- **Stub menu** — auto-created menu when POS sends an item Mise doesn't recognize (Decision #57).
- **Sales day** — วันขาย: the business day a sale belongs to, which is **not** the calendar day it happened on — a bill closed at 01:30 belongs to the night that started the evening before. Whenever the POS states the day, that statement is the truth and Mise never recomputes it from a timestamp: the POS decided it at shift close and every report the shop already argues over uses it. Only when a file carries times but no day does Mise derive one, using the branch's own cut-off (default 05:00). Stored as a plain date, never a timestamp to be truncated later.
- **Daily pulse** — พัลส์รายวัน: one number per branch per day — **what the customer paid**, i.e. after discount but INCLUDING VAT and service charge. Deliberately not [Gross profit]'s revenue, which excludes both: the pulse exists to be the same figure the owner sees in the POS app, and to be compared against `net + vat + service charge` summed from that day's detail. Optional, entered by whoever closes up, and **frozen once a detail file lands for that day** — from that moment its job is no longer to be read but to be the evidence the file is checked against, and a pulse anyone could edit to silence a warning would be no evidence at all. Its one irreplaceable use: a POS export that covered only part of a day is valid in every way Mise can check — every row real, header matching, no blank cells — and the pulse is the only witness that it is incomplete. See ADR 0020.
- **Delivery apps commission** — ค่า GP เดลิเวอรี: the cut a delivery platform (Grab, LINE MAN, foodpanda, Robinhood) keeps from an order, 25–32% depending on the programme the shop joined. It is an **expense**, never a deduction from revenue — the money is deducted from a monthly remittance, not from a bill, so subtracting it per line would mean guessing on every line and would leave the same dish carrying two different revenues depending on where it sold.
  ⚠️ **The Thai restaurant trade calls this "GP", which is NOT this project's GP.** In the trade, *"ค่า GP 30%"* means the platform's commission; in Mise, [Gross profit] means revenue minus cost of sales. Both meanings can appear on `/cost` at once, so the UI writes **"กำไรขั้นต้น"** in full for the Mise meaning and always pairs the other with the word เดลิเวอรี. The bare letters "GP" are never shown alone.
- **Gross profit** — กำไรขั้นต้น: revenue minus cost of sales. Revenue here means **after discount, excluding VAT, and excluding service charge** — VAT is collected on the Revenue Department's behalf and a service charge is paid out to staff as a labour expense (Decision #39), so counting either would inflate the figure with money that has no cost of sales behind it. See [Delivery apps commission] for the collision on the abbreviation.

## Cost Engine

- **Product cost** — what one **base unit** of a product costs at a **branch**, valued **FIFO**: the cost of the *next* unit that will be consumed, i.e. the front layer's. Measured per branch because two branches hold two physically different piles; rolled up to the business only as an explicit, labelled aggregate (see Tenant / Branch). Not stored anywhere — it is computed by replaying the stock ledger in `(occurred_at, created_at, id)` order on every read, which is what makes a backdated receipt or a voided one simply *correct* rather than something to repair. Note `cost × qty on hand ≠ inventory value`: a value figure sums the layers. See ADR 0014.
- **Cost layer** — one arrival of stock, carrying its base-unit quantity and **the money actually paid for it**. Consumption draws from the oldest layer first; a return cuts the layer it reverses rather than the oldest one. Stock consumed with none left forms a **negative layer** at the last known cost, which unwinds when goods next arrive. Layers exist only in memory during a read; the money — never a per-unit rate — is what the layer carries, so total value always equals money in minus money consumed, to the satang.
- **Cost declaration** — a signed, dated statement of what stock that arrived *without a document* cost (found during a recount, where the ledger records the quantity but no price). Append + supersede, like a supplier price: a correction never overwrites, it closes the previous one. Applies to found stock only — a received item's price belongs to its receipt. Corrects our knowledge of the past, so it takes effect at every date, including ones already reported.
- **Cost source** — where a cost figure came from, and therefore how far to trust it: the front layer · a human declaration · the last known purchase (stock is zero or negative) · unpriced (never purchased). The input Cost confidence is computed from.
- **Recipe** — สูตร: what one thing is made of. It makes **either** a [Menu] (a dish that is sold) **or** a PREPPED product (a [Production recipe]) — never both. Its ingredients point at **things** — a product or another menu — and never at a recipe *version*, so a set that contains a steak follows the steak's current recipe rather than the one it was written against. Versioned by append-and-supersede with an effective date, like a supplier price: an edit closes the previous version rather than overwriting it, which is what lets a period import post consumption against the recipe that was true on each past day. A new version is written only when the arithmetic changes. See ADR 0021.
- **Production recipe** — a [Recipe] whose output is a PREPPED product rather than a menu. The one notation that can hold **many inputs**, which is why chilli jam needs it and portioned salmon does not.
- **Set menu** — เซ็ทเมนู: a menu whose recipe ingredients are **other menus**. It needs no mechanism of its own — an ingredient may point at a menu, so a set is an ordinary recipe one level up. Changing the steak's recipe reaches every set containing it, because nothing was copied. Its revenue still lands on a single department; how 299 ฿ divides between kitchen and bar is an undecided calculation rule, and **not Part 22's** — the Ledger carries no department at all, so it waits for whoever builds revenue by department (ADR 0022 Q8).
- **Central recipe / branch recipe** — a recipe with no branch attached applies everywhere; a recipe attached to branches applies to those. A branch with nothing attached **has not decided anything**, so it follows the central recipe as it changes. The moment someone copies a recipe to a branch, that branch has declared it decides for itself and **nothing central reaches it again** — because a recipe change is a change to how a kitchen works, and one branch's cooks may be retrained while another's are not.
- **Recipe cost** — the sum of a recipe's ingredient costs at **one branch**, each RAW ingredient priced by [Product cost] and each PREPPED or menu ingredient expanded first. Not stored anywhere, for the same reason [Product cost] is not: a receipt keyed in late changes the past and there would be nothing to tell a stored figure it was now wrong. Memoised within a single request only, where nothing can change underneath it.
- **Cost confidence** — HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW on a recipe cost. Computed from the [Cost source] of every ingredient, and taken from the **weakest one, not an average**: any ingredient this branch has never bought makes the whole recipe LOW, and the screen names it and links to where its price can be declared. Averaging by cost share inverts itself — an unpriced ingredient is valued at zero, so it carries no weight, so the less is known about something the less the system claims it matters.
- **Cost cascade** — *(dissolved, ADR 0021 Q7)* — originally: a RAW cost change marks dependent recipes stale for recompute on read (H.9, Decision #54). Nothing is stored, so nothing can be stale; every read walks the recipe and replays the ledger fresh. The word is kept here only so a reader who meets it in the spec knows it was retired on purpose.
- **Draft recipe** — สูตรร่าง: a [Recipe] the shop is still deciding about, marked as a draft and **true on no day**. It is costed by exactly the same walk as a real recipe, so the figure in [Menu Lab] is the figure the recipe page would show — but it can never resolve for a sales day, never consume stock, and never blocks the published recipe of the same [Menu]. It may carry a **ราคาที่ตั้งใจ**, the price being considered while designing; that is a fact about the design, not a claim about today, so it never competes with the price read from sales, and once the dish has sales the sold price is the price. See ADR 0025.
- **Menu Lab** — the screen for a dish that has not happened yet: build a recipe, see its cost live, try a price against it. Cost needs a branch (ADR 0014 Q9), so it opens on the branch whose prices are freshest and says which one it used; [Cost confidence] travels with the number as everywhere else, because a dish nobody has bought the ingredients for is a guess and has to look like one.
- **Recipe coverage** — which [Menu]s have no [Recipe], ranked by **revenue** — the answer to "how much of my gross profit is currently guessed". Distinct from the coverage printed beside gross profit, which is about days already posted (ADR 0022). Rows carry a hint when another menu looks like the same dish, so a POS that reports ข้าวผัด three ways does not send someone to write the same recipe three times — a suggestion only, never a merge (ADR 0019's rule).
- **Recipe recursion depth** — max 5 levels (Decision #58), and **one budget shared by the whole walk** — products and menus alike, so เซ็ท → สเต็ก → สเต็กแล่ → เนื้อทั้งชิ้น spends four of it. A cycle may now run menu → menu, so the visited-set spans both kinds, and a refusal names the chain that overflowed rather than only saying it did.

## Architecture

- **RLS (Row-Level Security)** — Postgres feature enforcing tenant_id filter at DB layer. Defense-in-depth (H.10, Decision #55).
- **withTenantContext** — Prisma helper that sets `app.current_tenant_id` for RLS to use.
- **Tenant isolation** — guarantee that Tenant A cannot read/write Tenant B's data, even with bugged app code.
- **Branch override** — pattern where tenant-default settings can be overridden per-branch (Section A). First concrete use: **Supplier-Product Mapping** — `branchId` null = tenant default, `branchId` set = branch override; the branch-specific row **wholly replaces** the default for that branch (lookup tries branch-specific first, then falls back to the tenant default), not a field-level merge. See ADR 0009.
- **Permission triple-filter** — role × user_branch_access × user_department_assignment (H.4).
- **Materialized view freshness** — H.7: combine pre-computed mat view with live UNION for fresh + fast.

## Localization

- **Thai-first** — UI strings prefer Thai; code/comments in English.
- **THB** — Thai Baht (฿). Default currency.
- **Expense** — any baht leaving the business: rent, utilities, wages, services, **and materials**. A confirmed Goods Receipt creates its own expense (`source = FROM_GOODS_RECEIPT`), so purchases and overheads live in one table and the business-wide view has a single source of money-out. Fields that came from the receipt are not editable on it; the tax-invoice number, WHT and payment status are. `UNPAID`/`PAID` only — a partial payment describes an amount, and there is no payments module to hold one. See ADR 0016.
- **Recurring expense** — a template for something that repeats (rent, internet). It generates **nothing**: what is *due* is computed by asking which months have no expense carrying that template's id, and a human confirms each one — the default amount is a starting point, because an electricity bill differs every month. Nothing runs in the background, so "reminder" means visible when someone opens the page.
- **VAT** — Value Added Tax (Thailand: 7%). Charged *by* a supplier *to* the tenant on a purchase. Tenant can reclaim it only if VAT-registered (is_vat_registered); the supplier's VAT is recorded regardless. **Whether it is reclaimable is snapshotted onto each Goods Receipt** (`vat_reclaimable`), not read live: a shop that registers in October did really pay unrecoverable VAT all year, and that must not be re-valued retroactively. When it is NOT reclaimable it is part of what the stock cost (ADR 0016 Q2).
- **WHT** — Withholding Tax (Thailand-specific). Withheld *by* the tenant *from* its payment *to* a supplier (rate varies by type: service 3%, rent 5%). Opposite direction to VAT. **Computed on the amount BEFORE VAT**: 10,000 + 7% VAT withheld at 3% is 300, not 321, and the figure on the 50 ทวิ certificate must match what the recipient claims (ADR 0016 Q6 — master-spec §5.4's formula is wrong on this).
- **VAT/WHT decoupling** — VAT and WHT are independent: different directions, and neither is gated on tenant.is_vat_registered. A non-VAT-registered tenant still withholds WHT from supplier payments.
- **Tax invoice (ใบกำกับภาษี)** — required when VAT registered.
